
# Match Progression

The resource system is designed around matches lasting approximately **60–120 minutes**.

The economy should become operational quickly, with complexity emerging through territorial competition rather than through increasingly complicated resource management.

---

## Opening — approximately 0–15 minutes

Players establish:

- Scrap harvesting
- Alloy processing
- Basic Power
- Infantry production
- Light defenses

A nearby Scrap field should allow each player to establish a functioning economy almost immediately.

Harvesters collect Scrap and physically deliver it to the processor.

Because conversion is instant, the player receives Alloy immediately when the harvester arrives.

Players should begin encountering ordinary Oil resources during this phase or shortly afterward.

The opening should focus on establishing the first resource routes rather than building an elaborate industrial economy.

---

## Expansion — approximately 15–35 minutes

Players begin competing for:

- Larger Scrap fields
- Oil deposits
- Industrial Feedstock
- Data infrastructure
- Settlements
- Communications objectives

Vehicle production becomes increasingly important.

Players begin building Fuel processing infrastructure and securing transport routes.

More efficient fuel resources can begin appearing toward the edges or center of the map.

Data and Authority also begin influencing the match.

At this point the game transitions from **establishing an economy to competing for territory**.

---

## Midgame — approximately 35–70 minutes

All major systems are active.

Players now decide how heavily to invest in:

- Army production
- Additional harvesters
- Forward processors
- Technology
- Cyberwarfare
- Defensive infrastructure
- Authority abilities
- Territorial expansion

Starting Scrap fields should begin losing importance.

Players increasingly rely on contested ruins, industrial areas, and energy deposits.

Rare fuel resources such as Uranium become strategically significant.

Raiding harvesters and processors can meaningfully disrupt enemy production without requiring the destruction of an entire base.

---

## Late Game — approximately 70–120 minutes

The economy should increasingly encourage confrontation rather than passive accumulation.

Safe resources near starting locations are depleted or insufficient for large armies.

The most valuable remaining resource sites are located in contested territory.

High-efficiency fuel resources become particularly important because advanced mechanized armies require significant Fuel production.

Data and Authority also become increasingly powerful tools for breaking established defensive positions.

A late-game assault might therefore involve:

1. Using Data to disable radar or defenses.
2. Spending Authority to call in reinforcements.
3. Attacking enemy Power infrastructure.
4. Cutting off Scrap or Fuel deliveries.
5. Launching the main conventional assault.

Late-game warfare therefore combines:

**Economy + Territory + Intelligence + Strategic Support + Military Force**

rather than simply rewarding whichever player has accumulated the largest stockpile.

---

# Economic Philosophy

Each system answers a different strategic question.

**Scrap:**  
Where can I recover construction material?

**Alloy:**  
What can I afford to build?

**Fuel Sources:**  
Which energy-rich territories are worth controlling?

**Fuel:**  
How much mechanized military power can I produce?

**Data:**  
Do I improve my technology or interfere with the enemy?

**Authority:**  
Do I call for assistance now or strengthen my strategic position?

**Power:**  
Can my infrastructure support everything I have built?

The resulting economy remains understandable:

`Harvest → Deliver → Instant Conversion → Spend`

while the deeper strategy comes from **which territory the player controls, how safely resources can be transported, and what they choose to do with the resources once acquired**.
